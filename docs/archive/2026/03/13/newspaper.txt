* The Met’s 3D scans: high-fidelity digital repatriation or a museum of ghost files?

* Axe and the rejection of the 2GB dependency tree
  https://github.com/jrswab/axe

It trades the expansive flexibility of Python’s ecosystem for a brutal, necessary efficiency that challenges our current tolerance for architectural waste.

* The hardening of agentic infrastructure in Rust
  https://github.com/onecli/onecli

It highlights the growing tension between the rapid prototyping of LLM tools and the disciplined engineering required to keep them from leaking credentials.

* The ESP32 as an agentic host: Hardware constraints meet high-level reasoning

The risk lies in the accumulation of technical debt when high-level abstractions outpace the debugging capabilities of such limited silicon.

* DuckDB Benchmarks a $999 MacBook Against Warehouse-Scale Clusters—And Wins Some Rounds

* LLM Consistency & Documentation Trade-offs in Agent Workflows
  https://github.com/obsessiondb/rudel
  Short, focused sessions with clean context improve LLM task reliability—while overly detailed or outdated documentation (e.g., in `CLAUDE.md` or `AGENTS.md`) can introduce inefficiency. Users report ~50% inconsistency in skill execution, with 26% of sessions abandoned early. Simplifying documentation and isolating tasks into single-purpose sessions often yields better results than exhaustive, outdated instructions. Skepticism about unverified session data underscores the need for transparency in analytics. Quote: "Keeping context clean and focused is a highly effective way to keep the agent on task."

* Software Reliability as a Scaling Challenge
  https://quint-lang.org/posts/llm_era
  AI doesn't fundamentally change the engineering requirements for reliability; rather, it forces a shift in focus toward rigorous specification refinement and validation. Developers must now act as a high-velocity 'core team' managing a massive influx of automated contributions that require the same traditional testing and monitoring frameworks. Quote: AI now makes every product operate as if it has a vibrant open-source community with hundreds of contributions per day and a small core team with limited capacity.
