* The Abyss Between 'Bare Metal' Learning and Practical Routing
  https://nbailey.ca/post/router/
  The community highlights a fundamental divide in networking: the educational value of building a router from scratch using Linux kernel features (iptables, DHCP, and shell scripts) versus the operational stability of purpose-built hardware and specialized distributions like OPNsense. While modern x86 hardware is powerful enough to route, dedicated 'router-on-a-chip' devices remain superior for latency and power efficiency due to hardware-level packet forwarding. Quote: When I am doing network management on my weekends, I’m so glad I’m not stuck in the Linux terminal learning about networking internals and can instead just go to a webui.

* Copying as a Creative Foundation: Lessons from the Demoscene
  https://www.datagubbe.se/aipixels/
  The demoscene’s evolution reveals a nuanced truth about art: **copying is a fundamental step in learning**, but the community now emphasizes **originality through transparency** (e.g., work-in-progress stages as proof). While early roots in cracking culture normalized borrowing, modern norms—like Revision’s strict rules—reflect a shift toward **crediting influences and valuing process over product**. The tension between rebellion and craftsmanship underscores that **artistic growth begins with imitation but matures through authenticity**. Quote: "Every artist starts out by copying the works of others. It's how you learn."
